# PCB notes — retuned-quad variant

`pcb/esp-p148-3way-crossover-retuned-quad-pcb.json`, generated by
`tools/gen_pcb.py`. Board is **101.6 × 83.8 mm**, two layers, all through-hole.

![Board placement](../pcb/esp-p148-3way-crossover-retuned-quad-pcb.png)

## What this file is, and what it isn't

**It contains:** the board outline, four M3 mounting holes, all 44 footprints
placed and oriented, every one of the 117 pads assigned to its net, silkscreen
designators, and a bottom-layer ground pour.

**It does not contain routed signal traces.** Open it in EasyEDA and you get a
correct ratsnest over a considered placement — then route, by hand or with
EasyEDA's autorouter.

That split is deliberate. Placement is the part that needs judgement and can be
checked here: pad-to-net agreement with the schematic, courtyard overlaps,
coincident pads, parts inside the outline, and total ratsnest length are all
verified by the generator on every run. Routing is the part that needs a real
design-rule check against real clearances and a real manufacturer — which this
toolchain cannot do. A board with unverified copper on it is worse than a board
with none, because it looks finished.

## Importing

1. EasyEDA Std → **File → Open → EasyEDA…** and choose the `-pcb.json`.
2. Check the placement, move anything you don't like.
3. Route. `Route → Auto Router` handles a board this sparse easily, or route by
   hand in the order below.
4. Run **Design → Design Rule Check** before ordering, and set the clearances to
   whatever your fabricator requires. The file ships with 12 mil tracks and
   10 mil clearance, which is comfortable everywhere.

If you would rather start from scratch, open the schematic and use
**Convert → Convert Schematic to PCB** — you'll get the same netlist with the
footprints in a default pile, and none of the placement.

## How it's laid out

Signal flows left to right, filter 1 across the upper half and filter 2 across
the lower half, matching the schematic.

* **Left edge, top to bottom:** input (J2), then the input coupling network
  (C0, R1), then power (J1) and the output header (J3). Keeping all the
  off-board wiring on one edge keeps the loop areas small when it's boxed up.
* **Each quad sits in the middle of its own filter**, with its bypass caps hard
  against the package and level with the supply pins — C5/C6 on U1, C7/C8 on U2.
* **Right edge:** the frequency pots (VR1A/VR1B, VR2A/VR2B) and the test points
  (TP1, TP2), as 0.1" pads. The pots are meant to be panel-mounted and wired to
  these, not board-mounted — this is a test instrument, so the controls belong
  on the front panel.
* **Bottom layer is a ground pour**, which is why GND is excluded from the
  ratsnest figure below.

Resistor positions are chosen by the generator, not by designator order, so
R2 is not next to R3. Assignments are permuted within each functional group and
scored on total ratsnest length; the reported figure is the result:

```
ratsnest (excl. poured GND): 1663 mm grid placement -> 1422 mm optimised
```

## Routing order that matters

Route these first, and keep them short — the rest of the board is not fussy at
audio frequencies:

1. **The integrator capacitors** (C1/C2 on U1, C3/C4 on U2) back to their
   op-amp inverting inputs. These are the high-impedance nodes; long traces here
   pick up hum and stray capacitance shifts the tuning.
2. **The summing amp inverting inputs** — three resistors land on each
   (R2/R5/R6 into U1B, R12/R15/R16 into U2A). Keep that junction compact.
3. **The pot wiring** to VR1A/VR1B and VR2A/VR2B. Off-board wiring here is
   unavoidable, so use screened cable if the box is large, and keep both gangs'
   runs the same length so they stay matched.
4. Everything else, then pour ground last and re-pour after any edit.

## Things to check before you order

* **Footprints are generic.** The axial parts are on 0.4" pitch and the caps on
  5 mm; check them against the parts you actually have, especially the 68 nF
  film caps, which are often 7.5 mm pitch and physically larger than the box
  drawn here.
* **C0 is drawn as a 5 mm-pitch radial electrolytic** with pin 1 positive.
* **The quad pinout is the industry-standard 14-pin one** (V+ on 4, V− on 11).
  Verify against the datasheet for whichever part you buy.
* **This is one channel.** Build two for stereo.
* Bypass caps are about 11 mm from the supply pins, which is what a DIP-14 with
  mid-package power pins allows without going to the bottom side. That is fine
  at audio frequencies; if you want them tighter, move them to the bottom layer
  directly under each package.
